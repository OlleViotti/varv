<h1 align="center">Varv one</h1>

**Varv one** is a loop station that runs in the browser, built for the phone. You listen to the click and your loops in Bluetooth headphones and record with the phone's own microphone. Bluetooth delays everything you hear by a couple of hundred milliseconds, so anything you play along is recorded late. Varv corrects that: every recording is shifted back by a latency you set, or have the app measure for you, so it lands on the beat.

*Varv* is Swedish for lap, or one turn round the loop.

**Open it:** https://olleviotti.github.io/varv/. On an iPhone, open it in Safari, allow the microphone, and choose Add to Home Screen from the share menu. It then runs full screen, works offline, and keeps your saved projects.

## Guide

Audio in Settings starts with the setup in three steps, each ticked once done: Start audio, Measure exactly (the headphones) and Measure by tapping (the keys). Short guide lines on the play screen, in edit mode and in settings explain each part when it first becomes relevant, one short line at a time on the play screen, and each one disappears for good once you have used what it describes, or when you tap it. Settings has a button to show them again.

## The play view, and what slides over it

The top of the screen is a thin strip with the in meter on the left and the out meter on the right. Its bottom edge is amber while playing and the whole strip turns red while recording. Everything else slides over the play view and leaves a strip of it showing; tapping the strip or swiping back returns to play.

- **Hold Record** to start or stop. The record options are under **Behaviour** in Settings: Overdub with Record, Count-in, Lap after lap (off: Record records one lap) and Record into, the next empty track or a chosen one for the next recording.
- **Hold Undo** for the undo history: everything Undo, Delete, Reset or Empty removed, newest first, each with Bring back, so any of them can come back, not only the latest.
- **Free length is released as soon as it is not needed.** In Free mode, once no recording is left in use (undone, removed or its track deleted), the length is let go and the next recording sets a new one, free of the old. Bringing back a removed recording while nothing is recorded sets the loop to its length again; once a new loop has been recorded, recordings that do not fit it cannot be brought back.
- **Track length on the play screen.** A track without recordings has − and + in the lower corners of its strip: + adds one loop length (2, 4, 6, 8 bars on a 2-bar loop), − takes one away, and below the loop length halves it (1 bar). A length other than the loop's shows by the track number. Beats per bar, bars per lap and the length mode are fixed only while there are recordings in use; recordings removed and waiting in the undo bar no longer lock them, but once the loop changes they can no longer be brought back.
- **Accents.** Under Loop and click, the Accents switch shows one square per beat of the bar; tap a square to step it through normal, medium, strong and silent, and the click plays it so (three click sounds: normal, a middle accent, and the strong one). The beats are still counted evenly by the counter, quantize and the bar lines; only the click changes, and it can be changed while playing. For 7/8 + 3/4: 13 beats per bar, tempo in eighths, strong on 1 and medium on 3, 5, 8, 10 and 12. Saved with the project. Beats per bar go up to 32 and the tempo from 20 to 480 bpm.
- **The transport bar** is Start, Record, the circle, Capture last and Undo. The **circle** is the mark from the app icon, live: 40 spokes swing out from a middle circle with the output; while playing each spoke is the level when the playhead (the amber hand) last passed it, so the lap's sound shows as a ring, and stopped they follow the sound, dimmer. Its dot is dark when stopped, cream when playing, red when recording and blinking while armed; during a count-in the beats left show there. It stands on the bar's own background, a little larger than the buttons. Tap it to open **Loop and click**; when anything is open (a panel such as Loop and click or the undo history, the pads, Edit or Settings), a tap on it closes that instead (length, tempo, tap tempo, beats per bar, bars per lap, click and its volume) over whatever is on screen, the pads included; tap outside it or Done to close it. A short tap on Click still turns the click on or off.
- **The tempo can change after recording.** Sampler laps move their notes to the new tempo and keep their sound. Audio recordings are time-stretched without changing their pitch, always from the original recording, so changing back gives the original exactly; a note under the tempo says what that costs in sound quality, and a change of more than 12 % from where they were recorded brings a warning. The tempo is applied once the + and − taps have rested for a moment, not on every step. Beats per bar, bars per lap and the length stay fixed while there are recordings.
- **Speaker**, under Audio › Output, for playing a project out loud. While the microphone is on, an iPhone treats the page like a phone call and plays only to the earpiece or to headphones with a microphone (AirPods); Speaker turns the microphone off, so the sound can go to the loudspeaker, a Bluetooth speaker or AirPlay. A warning says that the microphone does not work while it is on. It is remembered. Sampler tracks still record what is played on the screen. (The list of outputs to choose from is gone: on iPhone it did nothing.)
- **Varv opens on Audio in Settings**, with **Start audio** at the top, marked with a soft amber glow; once the audio runs, the glow moves to Measure exactly and Measure by tapping (those not yet done). The marks go out as soon as you go elsewhere. The microphone and output can be checked here first; swipe right for the play view.
- **Play** is the live view. Each track has its own colour: on the left a tile with **M** above **S**, its coloured edge pulsing with the track's level; then a dark panel with the track's number and its waveform in its colour and the playhead (an empty track shows an empty panel, and a track you can't hear fades out); and on the right a matching tile with the sample button above the record button (on a track with microphone recordings, where the sample button is hidden, the record button fills it). Tracks are numbered by their place in the list, not named. A new project starts with four empty tracks; the tracks share the screen height and shrink as more are added, down to a minimum, after which the list scrolls.
  - **Microphone**: records a sample (tap again, or press Record, to stop), which makes the track a sampler. On a sampler the button shows a keyboard and opens its pads. It is hidden on a track that holds microphone recordings.
  - **M** (top of the tile) mutes the track and **S** (bottom) solos it, following the mute timing.
  - **●** records one lap into the track, starting at its next lap: the button blinks while it waits and turns red while it records, and the track says "next lap", then "recording". When stopped, playback starts with the count-in first. Tap it again to cancel.
  - **Swipe right on a track** (or touch it and slide right) to edit that track.
- **Track volume on the play screen:** hold a track's strip still for a moment, then slide right for louder or left for quieter; its volume (0 to 150 %) is shown on the strip while you slide, and the list does not scroll or swipe meanwhile.
- **Swipe right for Edit.** It slides in from the left, with Master and then the track numbers as tabs along its top, each in its track's colour as on the play screen; it opens on the track you last touched. On a sampler, the open track's tab shows a play sign over its number: tap it to hear the sample with its edits. Mute and solo are on the play screen. At the top of a track, pinned while you scroll, two rows of icons, the same on microphone tracks and samplers so an effect is always in the same place:
  - **Sound edits**, which change the recording itself: **Source** (on a sampler the sample's trim with a close-up of each edge, drawn, like the envelope, as the level from the bottom up: both swings of the wave in one, so the edges are easier to judge, New, Auto trim and Pads, the sustain loop, the **sample library** (save the sample under a name, with its trim, root, reverse, envelope, compressor and loop, or load a saved one in its place; the library is shared by all projects, each saved sample can be played and deleted there, and an empty track can load one too), and New track with this sample; a microphone track has no sample, but has **New sampler track from take N** for each of its takes, the counterpart of New track with this sample: the take as recorded becomes the sample of a new track just after it, trimmed to its sound; an empty track can record a sample here; and, for the track, **Remove the sample and laps** or **Remove the recordings**, which keeps every setting, and **Delete the track**, both asking Sure? while counting down 3, 2, 1, with the bar at the bottom able to bring back what they removed), **Shape** (the envelope on a sampler; on a microphone track the newest take drawn as recorded and after the compressor, with the threshold; and the compressor, worked out in advance on the sample or on each take so it can look ahead and nothing slips through), **Pitch** (octaves, semitones, fine tuning, on a sampler its root note, and autotune, with a picture of the intonation: the pitch of the sample or the newest take over its length as played, grey, and where autotune puts it, in the track's colour, over lines for the notes of the key), **Tricks** (reverse, tremolo, lo-fi, ring modulator and granular, and on a microphone track also half or double speed and tape stop) and **Laps** (the takes or laps, newest first with M, S and × on each, the latency for all of them, quantize on a sampler, and the track's bars).
  - **Live effects**, on what plays: **Mix** (volume, pan, filter, reverb and delay), **EQ**, **Drive** (distortion), **Effects** (colour: bit crush, ring mod, tremolo, telephone, radio, cut lows, mids or highs; and space: echo and reverb break, freeze, slice, reverse delay, ping-pong) and the **XY pad**. Five icons in each row.

  Tap an icon to open its page below the icons, tap it again to close it; a dot on an icon means something in it is switched on or changed. Which page is open, and how far down it is scrolled, is remembered for each track and for Master while you are there and for 30 seconds after you leave; after that it opens on Mix. A newly recorded sample opens Source for its first 30 seconds. Master has the same two rows: Mix, EQ, Delay, Reverb and Compressor, and below them, in the same places as on a track, Effects and XY pad. Double-tap any setting to return it to its default. Settings locked by recordings stay as they are.

  The **XY pad**, after Korg's Kaoss Pad, is a big square for playing effects live with one finger. The X axis sweeps one effect from left to right and the Y axis another from bottom to top (by default the filter, low-pass to high-pass, and reverb); each edge and corner can also have an effect that comes in as the finger nears it. The effects: filter, low-pass, high-pass, reverb, delay, volume down, pan, distortion, bit crush, ring mod and its pitch, tremolo and its speed, and every live sound trick: echo and reverb break, freeze, telephone, radio, cut lows, mids or highs, slice with its position and length, reverse delay and ping-pong delay. The pad adds to what the Sound tricks settings are. The live effects only exist while they are turned up: each is built when it is raised above zero and taken away again when it is back at zero (freeze and ping-pong once their sound has died away), and the master reverb is only connected while something sends to it, so effects that are not in use cost the phone nothing. Lifting the finger sets everything back, unless **Hold** is on (then **Let go** does). The mapping is kept per track.
- **Swipe left for Settings,** which slide in from the right, with a row of icons at the top: **Audio** (start audio, latency, microphone and output, restart and the audio log), **Loop** (loop and click), **Behaviour** (the record options: overdub, count-in, lap after lap and which track to record into; and when mute, solo and scenes take effect), **Projects** (the current project, the saved projects and the performance recordings) and **Help** (the guide and the keys). Varv opens on Audio; otherwise the page last open is remembered for 30 seconds after you leave, then Loop again.

## Latency correction

- **Per recording.** Every recording is shifted back by the latency in force when it was made. Each one keeps its own value, which you can nudge afterwards in steps of 1 or 10 ms while the loop plays, watching the hits line up with the beat lines.
- **Automatic measurement, exact.** The app plays ten clicks and listens for them through the microphone, using a matched filter tuned to the click, so no human timing is involved. Hold the phone's microphone against an earbud and tap Measure exactly. It finds a faint click buried in noise and reports nothing rather than a wrong value when it cannot hear the clicks.
- **Screen latency, for the sampler.** A note played on the screen is late by the headphone delay plus the time the touch takes to reach the app. It is 125 ms until measured. Measure it once with **Measure by tapping**: after a count-in of four higher clicks the app plays eight clicks and you tap along on the beat as you hear it. Nothing on the screen moves in time with the clicks, since the screen has a delay of its own, so you tap only to what you hear. If the audio or the microphone has only just started, it first waits until they have settled (an iPhone can still be switching its audio route), so the first clicks do not glitch. Taps far from the rest are dropped and the others averaged; the result says how much your taps scattered and how precise the value is. Touches are placed on the audio clock through the offset between the page's clock and the audio clock, measured by the app itself: the freshest of the last second of readings of the audio clock. The browser's own estimate of its output latency is not used, since on a phone with Bluetooth it is not known to stay put. It sits under the headphone latency in Settings and can be nudged in 1 ms steps; until it is measured it is guessed as the headphone latency plus 60 ms. The headphone latency starts at 185 ms; double-tap either value to return it to its default.
- **Restart audio**, under Audio in Settings, under Restart and more, closes the sound and the microphone completely and starts them again, as when the app opens; recordings, samples and settings stay. Try it if the delay grows, the latency measurements start to vary, or the sound goes to the wrong place; then measure the latency again.
- **Audio setup.** Every audio setting lives under **Audio** in Settings (the A key also opens it). In the order of setting up: **Start audio** (while the audio is not running), **Latency** (both measurements), then the microphone, its gain and low cut, and the output, each with a large meter, and a test sound; and **Restart and more** (restart audio, auto level recordings, what is in use and the audio log), all shown at once, without folding; the page scrolls clear of the transport bar. The output can be chosen where the browser allows it: directly in Chrome and Edge; on iPhone with iOS 26 the whole mix is then played through one media element whose output can be chosen, since Safari cannot choose the output of Web Audio itself, which adds a little delay; System default always plays directly. Elsewhere the system chooses, and on iPhone the output is changed from Control Center. Without headphones an iPhone plays through the earpiece while the microphone is on, because Safari records in the phone's call mode; choose the speaker on the audio page to hear it out loud. The microphones and the outputs are tappable rows with a tick on the one in use, in Settings too; iOS only allows the output to be switched inside a tap. The latency depends on the output, so measure it again after changing it.
- **Microphone choice.** The iPhone microphone is the default, even when headphones with a microphone are connected, and the app switches back to it if the system changes the input. On some iPhones and iPads, Safari moves the sound to the speaker when the phone's microphone starts; Varv then sets the audio session again, which brings the sound back to the headphones. `routetest.html` is a small page for checking how a device routes the sound. Another microphone, such as a USB interface, can be chosen in Settings.

## Loop length

- **Bars.** Tempo 40 to 240 bpm with tap tempo, 1 to 16 beats per bar and 1 to 32 bars per lap, with a click and a one-bar count-in.
- **Bars per track.** In Edit each track can have its own number of bars, for example a 4-bar melody over a 1-bar beat. It records on its own boundaries and its strip shows its own bars and playhead. The setting is fixed once the track holds a recording.
- **Free.** No tempo and no click. The first Record starts at once; play, then tap **End loop** to set the length (up to 30 seconds). Every track then has that length. Since nothing is playing during that first recording, it is not shifted for latency; later recordings are, so they land on the first one.

## Recording

- **One button.** Tap Record and it records lap after lap, each lap into the next empty track (or a new one), until you tap Record again; the button then reads **Finish**, which makes the lap in progress the last (pressed just as a lap ends, the lap just taken is the last); after that it reads **Keep going**, which takes it back. The track waiting for the next lap is marked **auto**. A lap with nothing played in it is thrown away and the same track waits for the next lap, so the tracks fill in order, top down. Hold Record to start or stop playback. This all works from a single pedal button.

- **A track's ● button** instead records exactly one lap into that track.
- **Capture last** keeps the lap you just played even if you never pressed Record.
- **Undo** removes the latest recording. Removed recordings and deleted tracks stay in a bar at the bottom with a Restore button until you dismiss it.
- **Levels.** Input gain (−12 to +30 dB) and an optional 80 Hz low cut sit before the recording. Recordings are kept at the level they were played; automatic levelling is an option. The in and out meters in the top bar turn amber near full scale, red on clipping or when the output limiter has to work hard. Each track shows its own level under its name.
- A live strip shows what the microphone hears, already shifted by the current latency.

## Tracks

- Add tracks before you start, name them and give them their own effects.
- **A track's ● button** records into it from its next lap. The Record button and the pedal fill the empty tracks from the top, then create new ones.
- Once a microphone track holds audio, the sample button on its right is replaced by an **overdub** button (**+●**): the lap it records plays on top of what is there. The **●** below it records a lap that replaces what was there, from the moment the new take starts playing; the earlier takes stay on the track, silent (switched off and dimmed in Edit), and Undo brings the previous one back. An overdub recorded after a replace layers on that replace. Each take has its own latency, reverse and remove.
- **Mute timing:** mute and solo act immediately by default. In Settings they can instead land on the next beat or the next bar (in free mode: the next pass of the loop), so you can tap a little ahead.
- Per track, in Edit: name, bars, volume, pan, graphical EQ, filter (darker or thinner), delay send and reverb send, plus the waveform and each take's latency, reverse and remove.
- **Distortion** per track, under Drive in Edit (off until switched on): Soft rounds the peaks like an overdriven tube, Hard clips them, Fuzz bends the two halves of the wave differently for a rougher sound. Drive goes up to +40 dB, and the output level is evened out so more drive gives more grit rather than more volume. Tone is a lowpass after it (800 Hz to 12 kHz), Mix blends in the clean sound and Level sets the output. It sits before the track's filter and EQ, is oversampled 4x so the new harmonics do not fold back, and works on microphone and sampler tracks alike.
- **Transpose** a track in Edit, under Pitch: octaves (±2), semitones (±11) and a fine tuner in cents (±50). The tempo and the timing stay the same. Every recording on the track is shifted, and the play screen shows the setting under the track name. Double-tap a value to reset it. The shift is computed in the background from the stored recording, so changing it loses nothing; the old version keeps playing until the new one is ready.
- **Autotune** a track in Edit, under Autotune: it pulls a single voice or instrument to the nearest note of a key and scale (all 12 notes by default, or major, minor or major pentatonic). **Speed** is how fast a note is pulled in (hard gives the robotic effect, slower keeps vibrato and slides) and **Amount** how far. The key is what you hear, after any transposing. A note is held until another is clearly closer, so a voice between two notes does not flicker. Chords confuse it.

## Sampler

A track becomes a sampler when a sample is recorded on it: tap the microphone button on an empty track's row (or **+ Sampler** on the pads), play or sing one sound, and tap again (or press Record) to stop; it stops by itself after 8 seconds. Reset in Edit makes it an empty track again. On a sampler, **New** in Edit records a new sample in its place.

- **Auto trim** cuts the sample as soon as it is recorded. The start goes back from the loudest point only to where the sound falls below a tenth of it, then 2 ms before the attack, so breaths and noise before the sound are cut; the end is where the level, averaged over 30 ms, is back down at the noise heard before the sound, plus 10 ms. The trim view shows the whole recording with the kept part lit; drag either edge to trim by hand, double-tap it or tap Auto trim to trim automatically again.
- **Pitch.** The pitch of the sample is detected when it is recorded, as a note with cents (YIN, as for autotune), so the keys play in tune from it. Sounds without a clear pitch, like most drums, count as C4. The root note can be moved by hand in semitones; double-tap it to go back to the detected one.
- **Edit options act on the sample.** On a sampler, the pitch (octaves, semitones and fine tuning) and autotune sit in the Sample section and are applied to the sample itself, not to each lap, and the sample can be reversed. Transposing keeps the sample's length and the keys move with it. Autotune on a sampler has two modes. **Whole sample** (the default) listens to the whole sample once, takes its pitch (after any transposing), and moves all of it by one fixed amount to the nearest note of the key and scale, scaled by Amount, so vibrato and slides stay as played. **Continuous** pulls each moment of the sample to the nearest note, as autotune does on a microphone track, so a note that wavers or slides is held in tune; Speed sets how fast it is pulled in (hard for the robotic effect) and Amount how far. The keys then count from the tuned note. Every lap is rendered from the processed sample, so a change is heard on all of them, and recording a new sample keeps the laps already played.
- **Envelope.** The volume over the length of the sample, drawn over its waveform like the graphical EQ, with the shaped waveform shown bright over the original. Drag **A** sideways for the attack, from nothing to 2 seconds, for a swell; **D** sideways for the decay and up or down for the sustain level, so a short decay to a low sustain gives a sharper attack; and **R**, in the box on the right, for the release: how long a Held note fades after you let go. **Punch** lifts the first moment after the attack above the recorded level, by up to +9.5 dB, falling back over 30 ms, for more attack than the recording has. Double-tap a handle to reset it. The envelope is applied to the sample after pitch and autotune, so it is heard live on the pads and on every lap, and a new sample recorded on the track keeps it. A note cannot last longer than the recording: the sustain holds at most to the end of the trimmed sample.
- **Compressor** for the sample, after the envelope settings: On, Threshold, Ratio (up to ∞:1), Attack, Release and Gain. It evens the sample out, pulling the loud parts down, and then brings the whole sample back to its own loudness, so the body and tail come forward; it shows how far the loudest part is pulled down. Because the sample is processed in advance it looks ahead: the gain starts coming down Attack before a loud part, so no peak slips through and the peaks stay within full scale. It comes after pitch and autotune and before the envelope, so the envelope you draw shapes the compressed sound (Punch then gives a harder attack). Saved with the project and kept when a new sample is recorded on the track or copied to a new track.
- **Notes:** Full plays the whole sample on every note; Held keeps it going for as long as the key is held and stops it when you let go, fading over the envelope's release. To keep it going, a **sustain loop** is chosen by itself in the strong, steady part of the tone: after the attack and decay and only where the level is still at least half its peak, so a faint tail is never picked; about 250 ms (at most half that part), a whole number of the tone's own cycles, placed where its end matches its start best and the level is the same at both ends, and crossfaded so the jump back is seamless. **Sustain loop**, under the trim view, shows it over the sample as it sounds: drag the band to move it or its edges to resize it (the length then snaps to whole cycles and the start to where it joins best), double-tap for the automatic choice. It is also shown shaded, marked loop, in the trim view while Held is on. It works best on steady tones (a sung vowel, an organ, strings); a plucked or struck sound fades within the loop and pulses gently. Chosen on the pads.
- **Fine trim:** right under the trim view, close-ups of the Start and End edges, 150 ms either side; drag one to move it finely (half the finger's travel), double-tap for where Auto trim puts it. **Reverse** sits with the trim view.
- **The pads** come up from the bottom when you tap a sampler's keys button on the play screen. Tabs along the top switch between the sampler tracks, and **+ Sampler** makes a new one (the first empty track, or a new track) and offers to record its sample right there. Below them are **Rec lap**, which records that track from its next lap, replacing its laps, and **+ Overdub**, which records one on top of them (it reads Cancel while waiting, and Finish or Keep going while you record with the Record button), **Full** or **Held** notes, and **Octaves**, from 0 to 4: at 0 the pads are **one big pad** that plays the sample as recorded, otherwise **a keyboard** of that many octaves stacked in rows, highest at the top, with the root note marked (the strip of a white key between two black keys does not play, so a finger aiming for a black key cannot catch it); **From** sets the lowest key: − and + move it between the Cs and Fs, so no black key is cut off at an edge; double-tap for where it starts. A sample with no clear pitch starts on the big pad. Every finger plays its own note and sliding along the keys plays each one. The transport stays at the bottom; swipe down, tap the play view above the sheet, or tap Done to close it.
- **Recording what you play.** Rec lap, or the track's ● button, records one lap of what you play on the screen, from its next lap. With the pad screen open, Record records lap after lap into the sampler, each lap replacing the last (the earlier ones kept, silent), or on top of it with **Overdub with Record** on in Settings, which applies to the big Record button everywhere, and laps with nothing played are thrown away; Capture last keeps the lap you just played. While a recording is on (waiting for its lap, counting in or recording) the pads are silent, and framed in red, since anything heard live would be late; each note is placed at its touch time minus the screen latency and the lap plays in time from the next lap on. Otherwise the pads sound at once, to try the sample out.
- **Quantize**, at the top of a sampler's Laps in Edit, moves where each played note starts towards the nearest 1/4, 1/8, 1/8 triplet, 1/16, 1/16 triplet or 1/32 note, by Amount (less keeps some of the feel). The notes as played are kept, so Off brings them back, and the tempo can still change. Not in free length mode.
- **New track with this sample**, under a sampler's name at the top of Edit, makes a new track with the same sample and all its settings, without the laps.
- Each lap keeps the screen latency it was recorded with and can be nudged afterwards, like a take. The sample and the notes of every lap are saved with the project.

## Graphical EQ

Each track and the master have a three-band EQ drawn as its response curve. Drag the L, M or H handle sideways to set the frequency and up or down to set the level, from −12 to +12 dB; double-tap a handle to return it to its default level and frequency. The low and high bands are shelves and the mid band is a bell. The curve is the real response of the filters, the filter setting included.

## Scenes

Five scene buttons, A to E, sit just above the five transport buttons. Hold one to store which tracks are muted and soloed; tap it to bring that back. By default a scene takes effect when the lap you are hearing ends, so you can pick the next part ahead; its button blinks until then. Under Settings, Scenes, it can instead act immediately or on the next bar. A scene that matches the current state lights up. Scenes are saved with the project.

## Master effects

Under Master in the Edit view:

- Volume, graphical EQ and filter.
- Delay: time as 1/16, 1/8, dotted 1/8 or 1/4 note, or free in milliseconds; repeats, level and mix send.
- Reverb: size, level and mix send.
- Compressor: threshold and amount. The click goes through a matching neutral compressor, so click and tracks are delayed by the same few milliseconds and timing is unaffected.
- Sound tricks and XY pad, as on a track but on the whole mix, after the EQ: the live tricks (bit crush, ring mod, tremolo, breaks, freeze, telephone, radio, cut lows, mids or highs, ping-pong). Slice and Reverse delay set on the master act on every track. The tricks that change the recordings themselves (reverse, speed, tape stop, lo-fi, granular) are per track only, and the master's pad has no pan or distortion. They are saved with the project.

## Projects

- **Everything is saved.** The first recording creates a project named after the date and time, and every change after that is saved automatically: tracks, takes, track settings, scenes, loop length, tempo and master effects. Rename it under Settings. After a reload, a Continue button reopens the last session.
- The raw recordings are stored, not the finished loops, so the latency of every take can still be changed after a project is reopened.
- Projects are stored in the browser on the device (IndexedDB) and are not synced anywhere.
- **Performance recording.** Records everything you hear, without the click, as an audio file. With the microphone included, what you play live is in it too, lined up with the loops by delaying the music by the latency. Recordings are kept under Settings, where you can save or share them.
- Export as a WAV file of the audible tracks, without effects or pan. With tracks of different lengths, the export runs until they all line up again.

## Live use

- The screen stays awake while playing. Large transport buttons: Start, Record, the circle (loop and click), Capture last and Undo.
- Keys, and Bluetooth pedals that send them: <kbd>Space</kbd>, <kbd>R</kbd>, <kbd>Enter</kbd> or <kbd>→</kbd> work like the Record button (tap to record, hold to start or stop), <kbd>←</kbd> or <kbd>Backspace</kbd> undo, <kbd>C</kbd> capture last, <kbd>K</kbd> click, <kbd>1</kbd>–<kbd>9</kbd> mute a track and <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>1</kbd>–<kbd>9</kbd> solo it, <kbd>E</kbd> edit, <kbd>S</kbd> settings, <kbd>Esc</kbd> back to play, <kbd>P</kbd> performance recording.

## Technical notes

Everything lives in `index.html`, with no build step and no dependencies apart from two web fonts. Recording runs in an AudioWorklet that writes the microphone into a ring buffer indexed by the audio clock, which is what makes sample-accurate alignment possible. The page must be served over https for the microphone to work. `sw.js` makes the app work offline.

A touch on the screen is placed on the audio clock from the event's own time stamp, through the offset between `performance.now()` and the audio clock's `currentTime`. `currentTime` moves once per audio buffer, so a reading is never ahead of the true offset, only behind it; the freshest reading of the last second is used. `getOutputTimestamp()` is not used, since its offset on a phone with Bluetooth is not known to stay put. Any fixed offset left in the mapping is part of the screen latency measured by tapping, so it cancels out. The sampler keeps a log of these events, and a lap of notes collects from it everything played in its window, like a recording reads the microphone ring.

The transposer and the autotune share one engine: pitch detection with YIN, and a phase vocoder with phase locking whose ratio can change over time, processed as a loop so the seam stays smooth, followed by resampling; it runs in a Web Worker. Alignment, measurement, the gestures, the EQ, the transposer, the sampler, projects and the track features are verified with automated tests in Chrome using simulated audio.
